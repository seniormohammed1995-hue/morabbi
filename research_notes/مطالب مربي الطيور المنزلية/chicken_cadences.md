# Recurring care tasks and check cadences for chickens (broilers, layers, backyard/baladi)

Scope note: primary sources actually read in full text were the **Ross Broiler Pocket Guide (Aviagen, 2015 EN)**, the **Hy-Line Brown Commercial Layers Management Guide (International Standards, December 2025)**, the **Hy-Line Technical Update "Vaccination Recommendations"**, and **University of Arkansas Avian Advice, July 2026, "Managing Heat Stress in Commercial Poultry Operations" (Dr. Zac Williams)**. Baladi/dual-purpose birds have no breed guide; values for them are inferred from layer/backyard guidance and should be `confidence: 'low'`.

## 1. Water: drinker checks, cleaning, intake as health indicator, water:feed ratio, hot weather

### Takeaway
Water must be available 24 h/day, and the water:feed ratio should be checked **daily**. Normal is about 1.6–1.8 L water per kg feed at 21°C (Ross), about 2:1 as a rule of thumb, and 3–4:1 under heat stress (U. Arkansas). A sudden drop or a significant shift in intake is an early warning sign of disease or equipment failure and suits a "compare to own baseline" alert.

### Cited Findings
- Birds should have access to clean, fresh, good-quality drinking water 24 hours a day. — [Ross Broiler Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- "Monitor the feed to water ratio daily." At 21°C (70°F), birds are drinking enough when water (L) : feed (kg) stays close to **1.8:1 for bell drinkers, 1.7:1 for nipples with cups, 1.6:1 for nipples without cups**. The ratio may be higher for the first few days. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Ideal drinking water temperature: **15–21°C (59–70°F)**. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Provide **supplementary drinkers for the first 4 days**. **Adjust drinker heights daily**. Space: nipples at 12 birds/nipple (<3 kg) or 9 birds/nipple (>3 kg); bell drinkers at 10 × 40 cm per 1000 birds. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Water sanitation: Ross gives chlorination at **3–5 ppm free chlorine** (measured at the drinker end of the line; the text was truncated in extraction). Hy-Line gives **2–4 ppm free chlorine at pH 5–7 (ORP 650–750 mV)**. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf); [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)
- Ross lists "Water and feed consumption" and "Daily and weekly mortality" as data-trend tools for recognising disease. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- "Poultry will consume twice as much water as feed under normal conditions, heat stressed birds will consume 3 to 4 times as much water." — [U. Arkansas Avian Advice, July 2026](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/about/Avian%20Advice%20July%202026.pdf)
- UGA's poultry drinking water primer says a potential problem may exist if there is a sudden change in water intake. Acceptable drinking water pH is 6.5–7.8 (search summary). — [UGA Bulletin 1301](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/B1301/)
- Cobb notes that a significant shift in the water:feed ratio signals that something has happened with the water equipment or flock status. Cobb does not recommend supplying water on fixed ratios (search summary via an aggregator, not read in the Cobb guide itself). — [The Poultry Site: monitor water consumption](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/articles/flock-management-tips-for-broilers-monitor-water-consumption-to-understand-your-broiler-flock/p2)
- Drops in water consumption often show up before mortality, disease signs or production loss (industry article citing UGA work, so treat as secondary). — [Engormix: water consumption](https://en.engormix.com/poultry-industry/articles/water-consumption-t35248.htm)
- Weekly cleaning and disinfection of waterers is listed for backyard hens in a municipal hen-keeping checklist (not extension, so treat as guidance). — [City of Leduc hen checklist](https://www.leduc.ca/sites/default/files/015050%20Hens%20Checklist.pdf)

### Inferences
- Suggested reminders: "check/refill water" **at least twice daily**, plus extra checks in hot weather (inferred from 24 h access, heat raising intake 3–4×, and the twice-daily rounds in brooding). "Clean drinkers" **weekly** (low confidence). "Record water use" **daily** where it can be measured.
- Alert rule: a drop in daily water use of more than X% against the flock's own trailing average. No authoritative source gives the percentage. Cobb/Ross support "sudden change" without a number, so any threshold (e.g., 10%) must be `confidence: 'low'` and recorded in DATA_REVIEW.md.

### Gaps
- No extension or breed source found giving a numeric "% drop in water intake = alert" threshold.
- No authoritative backyard drinker-cleaning frequency was found (only a municipal checklist).

## 2. Brooding: temperature by age, behaviour checks, humidity

### Takeaway
Broilers (Ross): about 30°C at chick height for whole-house brooding (32°C at the brooder edge for spot brooding) at 60–70% RH, falling to about 20–22°C by days 24–27. Measure temperature and RH **twice daily for the first 5 days, then daily**, and use chick behaviour as the final check. Layers (Hy-Line Brown): 35–36°C (floor) for days 0–3, stepping down about 2–3°C per week to 21°C by days 36–42.

### Cited Findings
- Pre-heat houses for **at least 24 h** before placement. At placement: air **30°C (86°F)** at chick height (whole-house), litter **28–30°C**, RH **60–70%**. Spot brooding: **32°C at the brooder edge**. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf); spot figure from search summary of [Ross Pocket Guide 2020](https://aa-intl.aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/Ross_Broiler/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2020-EN.pdf)
- Ross dry-bulb temperature (°C) needed for equivalent temperature at a given RH (ideal-RH values are highlighted red in the guide; colour was not recoverable in the extracted text):

  | Age (d) | 40% RH | 50% | 60% | 70% | 80% |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | 36.0 | 33.2 | 30.8 | 29.2 | 27.0 |
  | 3 | 33.7 | 31.2 | 28.9 | 27.3 | 26.0 |
  | 6 | 32.5 | 29.9 | 27.7 | 26.0 | 24.0 |
  | 9 | 31.3 | 28.6 | 26.7 | 25.0 | 23.0 |
  | 12 | 30.2 | 27.8 | 25.7 | 24.0 | 23.0 |
  | 15 | 29.0 | 26.8 | 24.8 | 23.0 | 22.0 |
  | 18 | 27.7 | 25.5 | 23.6 | 21.9 | 21.0 |
  | 21 | 26.9 | 24.7 | 22.7 | 21.3 | 20.0 |
  | 24 | 25.7 | 23.5 | 21.7 | 20.2 | 19.0 |
  | 27 | 24.8 | 22.7 | 20.7 | 19.3 | 18.0 |

  Chicks from donor flocks younger than 30 weeks need a start temperature **+1°C** above this. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- "Monitor temperature and relative humidity regularly (**twice daily in the first 5 days and daily thereafter**)". Check automatic equipment against manual readings at chick level, and calibrate at least once per crop. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- After placement, leave chicks to settle for 1–2 h, then check feed, water, temperature and humidity **after 1–2 hours** and adjust. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Crop-fill targets (% of chicks with full crop): **2 h: 75%; 8 h: >80%; 12 h: >85%; 24 h: >95%; 48 h: 100%**. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Vent temperature: measure on at least 5 chicks from several locations. The ideal is **39.4–40.8°C** during the first 4–5 days. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Behaviour, per Ross: evenly spread means correct. Chicks grouped under heaters means too cold (increase temperature and/or RH). Chicks crowded at walls or away from heat, and/or panting, means too hot (decrease temperature and/or RH). — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Post-brooding RH should be **50–60%**. RH **<50%** during brooding affects growth. At **>29°C with >70% RH**, growth is affected. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Hy-Line Brown brooding air temperature (floor / cage): **0–3 d: 35–36 / 33–36°C; 4–7 d: 33–35 / 30–32; 8–14 d: 31–33 / 28–30; 15–21 d: 29–31 / 26–28; 22–28 d: 26–27 / 23–26; 29–35 d: 23–25 / 21–23; 36–42 d: 21°C**. Light intensity is 30–50 lux for days 0–7, 25 lux for days 8–28, and 10 lux for days 29–42. — [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)
- An unofficial copy of a Hy-Line guide says: pre-heat 24 h (normal climate), 48 h (cool) or 72 h (cold). Brood at 60% RH, and for each 5-point RH rise above 60%, lower the temperature by 1°C (search summary of a Scribd upload, so unofficial). — [Scribd copy of Hy-Line manual](https://www.scribd.com/document/437452781/Hyline-Manual)

### Inferences
- App reminders: "brooder temperature + chick spread check" **twice daily from day 0 to day 5**, then **daily** until heat is removed (about day 21–28 for broilers, about day 42 for layer-type/baladi chicks). On day 0, a one-off "1–2 h after arrival: check crop fill, water, temperature" task, plus crop-fill checks at 24 h and 48 h.
- Baladi chicks: no breed guide exists. Use the layer (Hy-Line) curve as a low-confidence default because baladi birds are slower-growing and feather more like layer types (inference).

### Gaps
- The current Ross 2025 handbook and Cobb guide were not read, so there is no cross-check of the 2015 table.
- Hy-Line hour-by-hour brooding light hours could not be reliably matched to ages from the extracted PDF table. It lists 20 h or an intermittent program in the first period, then 18, 16, 14 and 12 h, but the row mapping is ambiguous.

## 3. Heat stress and cold stress

### Takeaway
No single authoritative °C cutoff exists. Heat stress depends on temperature **and** humidity, and can start at about 26–27°C (78–80°F) when humidity is high. Panting is the first visible sign, and wing spreading follows. Ross's concrete number is growth impairment at >29°C plus >70% RH. Alerts should combine temperature and RH (a THI-type rule) and the triggering values must be marked as guidance.

### Cited Findings
- "Panting is often the first visible indicator of this condition". Elevated RH "reduces the efficiency of latent heat loss, making panting less effective". Each °F of evaporative cooling can raise RH by about **2.5%**. Uneven bird distribution indicates problems with airflow, temperature or equipment. — [U. Arkansas Avian Advice, July 2026](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/about/Avian%20Advice%20July%202026.pdf)
- Heat-stressed birds drink **3–4×** as much water as feed (normal 2×). — [U. Arkansas Avian Advice, July 2026](https://poultry-science.uark.edu/about/Avian%20Advice%20July%202026.pdf)
- At **>29°C (84.2°F) and >70% RH**, broiler growth is affected. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Heat stress onset in poultry can be as low as **78–80°F (about 25.5–26.7°C)** depending on humidity (extension source, search summary). — [NC State Extension, Orange County, 2025](https://orange.ces.ncsu.edu/2025/07/the-signs-of-heat-stress-in-poultry-and-small-ruminant-animals-tips-for-keeping-your-animals-cool/)
- Spreading of wings is a listed sign of heat stress. — [University of Guam Extension poultry welfare fact sheet 2026](https://www.uog.edu/_resources/files/extension/2026-uog-extension-fact-sheet-poultry-welfare-4-1-26.pdf)
- For layers, an **acute** rise in THI increases mortality more than a gradual rise at similar temperature and humidity (peer-reviewed study), which argues for alerting on sudden heat spikes. — [Frontiers in Veterinary Science 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fvets.2020.568093/full)
- Hy-Line energy rule: for each **0.5°C** above or below **22°C**, subtract or add about **2 kcal/bird/day**, which shows 22°C as the layer reference temperature. — [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)
- Cold signs in chicks: grouping under heaters (see section 2). — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Alert tiers (guidance, low confidence): "watch" at ≥27°C with RH ≥70%, or ≥30°C at any RH; "act" at ≥29°C with RH >70% (Ross), or when panting or wing spreading is observed. Actions: extra water checks, cool water (target 15–21°C), shade and ventilation. These tiers are synthesised, not lifted from one source.
- The project already holds weather coefficients in `src/data/weather.ts`, so thresholds belong there with `confidence: 'low'`.

### Gaps
- No primary source with a published poultry THI formula and cutoffs was read in this pass.
- Specific heat-stress actions (feed withdrawal timing during the hottest hours, electrolytes) were not confirmed from primary text.
- Cold-stress thresholds for adult backyard birds were not found.

## 4. Feed: feedings per day, phase changes, mould checks

### Takeaway
Broilers (Ross): starter 0–10 d (up to 14 d if behind weight), grower 11–25 d, finisher after 25 d, and an extra finisher if kept past 42 d. Adjust feeder height **daily** and let birds clear the feeders **once daily**. Layers (Hy-Line Brown) change diet by **body weight**, not age. Pre-lay starts no earlier than 15 weeks and must stop at first egg.

### Cited Findings
- Ross feed phases: **Starter 0–10 d** (can extend to 14 d if target weights are not met), **Grower 11–25 d**, **Finisher >25 d**. "Broilers fed beyond 42 days of age will require an additional finisher feed." Full pellets (3–4 mm) are not given before 18 d. Birds should be on the main feeding system by 6–7 d. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- "Adjust feeder height daily" (breast level). "Allow the birds to clear the feeders once daily." Diets "should be regularly sampled". — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Whole-grain inclusion: starter 0, grower gradually up to 15%, finisher gradually up to 20%. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Hy-Line Brown diet change body weights: **Starter 1 → Starter 2 at 186–197 g; → Grower at 456–482 g; → Developer at 1046–1105 g; → Pre-lay at 1246–1317 g; → Layer at 1357–1434 g** (the pairing of each weight with its transition is inferred from table order). "Do not feed Pre-Lay Diet earlier than 15 weeks", nor later than the first egg (it has too little calcium). If pre-lay is not used, raise developer calcium to 1.4%. Body weight at 17 weeks is 1357–1434 g. — [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)
- Withdrawal feed is needed when regulated pharmaceutical feed additives are used, and local legislation sets the withdrawal time. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- Reminders: "feed phase change" at broiler day 11 and day 26 (and day 43 if still kept). Layer phase changes are best triggered by a weighing result, with weeks used as a fallback.
- A "check feed for mould/caking/smell" task when refilling or weekly is sensible, but no source was found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No authoritative source found on the number of feedings per day for backyard flocks, or on mould-check frequency and storage.
- Lohmann and Cobb phase tables were not read.

## 5. Egg collection and nest boxes

### Takeaway
Extension sources agree: collect **at least twice daily** (once daily is the absolute minimum). Most eggs are laid by about 10:00. Add a third collection in very hot or cold weather.

### Cited Findings
- Collect at least twice daily, preferably before noon, with a third collection in late afternoon or early evening in hot or cold weather (search summary). — [Colorado State Extension: home-produced chicken eggs](https://foodsmartcolorado.colostate.edu/food-safety/home-community-gardens-and-livestock/home-produced-chicken-eggs)
- Most flocks lay the majority of eggs by **10:00 a.m.**; collect at least twice daily, especially in extreme temperatures (search summary). — [Penn State Extension](https://extension.psu.edu/proper-handling-of-eggs-from-hen-to-consumption)
- Once daily is the minimum and twice daily is better (search summary). — [Extension Foundation: Safe handling of eggs from small and backyard flocks](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/food-safety-with-poultry-meat-and-eggs/safe-handling-of-eggs-from-small-and-backyard-flocks/)
- Collect at least once a day, preferably twice, and keep floor eggs separate from clean nest eggs (search summary). — [Alabama Extension: egg quality and safety in small flocks](https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/poultry/enhancing-egg-quality-safety-in-small-flocks/)
- In winter extremes, a third or more collection may be needed to prevent freezing (search summary). — [University of Alaska Extension](https://uaf.edu/ces/publications/database/agriculture-livestock/safe-egg-handling.php)
- Nests need deep, clean bedding, with at least 4 boxes for flocks of up to 15 hens (search summary, aggregated across the extension sources above, so the exact origin is unverified).

### Inferences
- Reminders: egg collection at about 10:00–11:00 and about 16:00, plus a third collection when the weather alert is active. Nest bedding check weekly (low confidence).

### Gaps
- No extension source found giving a specific nest-box bedding replacement interval.

## 6. Litter, coop cleaning, ammonia

### Takeaway
The best numeric anchors are ammonia (<10 ppm ideal; >20 ppm raises respiratory risk; ≥25 ppm reduces growth) and litter moisture of 20–25%. The squeeze test works for an app: a ball that stays tight means too wet. Backyard cleaning frequencies come only from non-extension sources.

### Cited Findings
- Ammonia: ideal **<10 ppm**; smell detectable at **≥20 ppm**; >10 ppm damages lung surface; >20 ppm increases susceptibility to respiratory disease; >25 ppm may reduce growth. CO₂ ideal <3000 ppm (>3500 ppm causes ascites). CO ideal 10 ppm (>50 ppm harmful). — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Litter moisture should be maintained at **20–25%**. Litter that "adheres tightly and remains in a ball" is too wet, and slightly adhering litter is correct. Ammonia at 25 ppm depresses growth and worsens feed conversion (search summary). — [UGA Bulletin 1267: Litter quality and broiler performance](https://fieldreport.caes.uga.edu/publications/B1267/litter-quality-and-broiler-performance)
- Wet litter favours coccidial oocyst proliferation and coccidiosis outbreaks. Prolonged RH >70% causes caking (search summary of an industry article). — [WATTAgNet: to control coccidia, control litter moisture](https://wattagnet.com/articles/38226-to-control-coccidia-control-litter-moisture)
- Coccidia damage the gut and produce wet droppings (search summary). — [UF IFAS VM020](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/VM020/pdf)
- Cleaning between flocks (Ross): disinfectant contact for at least 4 h; formalin must be <2 ppm before re-entry. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Backyard (non-extension, low confidence): **daily** removal of soiled bedding and spilled feed or water, plus record-keeping; **weekly** waterer cleaning, coop inspection, and litter and dust-bath check. — [City of Leduc hen checklist](https://www.leduc.ca/sites/default/files/015050%20Hens%20Checklist.pdf). Deep clean of the coop about every 6 months (retail and popular sources only, such as Tractor Supply and Dummies, which do not meet the project's source rules).

### Inferences
- Alerts: "ammonia smell noticeable" (≈20 ppm by Ross's statement, so action needed) and "litter forms a ball when squeezed" → change or turn wet litter, especially around drinkers. Weekly litter check, daily spot-clean under the roost (low confidence).

### Gaps
- No extension-grade backyard coop deep-clean or litter-change interval found. This needs DATA_REVIEW.md if used.
- Bloody droppings as a coccidiosis sign was not confirmed from a primary text in this pass.

## 7. Lighting programs by age

### Takeaway
Broilers (Ross): 23 h light / 1 h dark for days 0–7, then 4–6 h of darkness. Change gradually over 2–3 days. Intensity 30–40 lux to day 7, then ≥5–10 lux, with <0.4 lux in the dark period. Layers (Hy-Line Brown): step down to a constant short day in rearing, then light-stimulate at about **1350 g** body weight, adding **30 min** at a time up to **16 h**.

### Cited Findings
- Ross: 0–7 d **23L:1D**; after 7 d, **4–6 h darkness** in one continuous block of at least 4 h if intermittent. Changes over **2–3 days**. Intensity **30–40 lux to 7 d**, at least **5–10 lux after**, dark period **<0.4 lux**. Local legislation applies. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Hy-Line Brown: light stimulation at **1350 g** (for egg numbers) or **1380 g** (for egg weight), increasing light "in 30-minute increments until reaching 16 hours". Light intensity 30–50 lux for days 0–7, 25 lux for weeks 2–4, 10 lux for weeks 5–6. The rearing step-down table shows about 10 h during rearing, rising to 16 h around point of lay. — [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)
- Hy-Line Brown Alt: closed houses reduce light 2 h/week to 10 h by week 7; open houses 12 h following natural day length. Stimulate at about 1.35 kg with 85% uniformity (about 16 weeks); if uniformity <80%, wait until 1.40 kg (search summary). — [Hy-Line TU HLB10](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20HLB10%20ENG.pdf)

### Inferences
- In backyard and baladi flocks under natural light, the actionable reminder is about weeks 16–18: "do not increase day length before target weight". In winter, optional supplemental light up to 16 h for layers (low confidence for baladi).

### Gaps
- Exact Hy-Line hours for 0–6 weeks could not be cleanly mapped (see section 2).

## 8. Weighing and uniformity

### Takeaway
Weigh **at least weekly**, at the same time of day, from at least 3 locations. Broilers 0–21 d are bulk-weighed with at least 100 birds or 1% of the flock, whichever is larger. Report the mean and CV%. Layer light stimulation depends on uniformity (≥85% ideal, <80% means delay).

### Cited Findings
- Weigh regularly at the same time of day, with equal samples from at least 3 locations. Days 0–21: bulk weighing of at least 100 birds or 1% of the population (whichever is larger). Cross-check auto-weighers manually **at least once per week**. Re-sample if the data are inconsistent. Weekly records: average live weight and **weekly uniformity (CV%)**. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- CV% = (standard deviation ÷ mean) × 100. The lower the CV%, the more uniform the flock. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)
- Layer uniformity of 85% at light stimulation, and if <80%, delay to 1.40 kg (search summary). — [Hy-Line TU HLB10](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20HLB10%20ENG.pdf)
- Hy-Line notes a 10–12% body weight loss at transfer to the laying house due to reduced water intake. — [Hy-Line Brown STD Dec 2025](https://www.hyline.com/filesimages/Hy-Line-Products/Hy-Line-Product-PDFs/Brown/BRN%20STD%20ENG.pdf)

### Inferences
- Reminder: "weekly weigh-in" on the flock's age day (day 7, 14, 21 and so on). For small home flocks, weigh all birds or a fixed sample. The 100-bird minimum does not apply at home scale (inference).

### Gaps
- A target CV% value was not extracted from the Ross text.

## 9. Daily observation checklist and mortality

### Takeaway
Ross treats daily assessment of behaviour, appearance, environment and clinical signs, together with daily and weekly mortality and water and feed trends, as the core disease-recognition routine. Extension guidance adds the backyard signs to watch: hunched posture, ruffled feathers, closed eyes, off feed, isolation, and abnormal droppings. Multiple sudden deaths call for an immediate report to a vet or the authorities.

### Cited Findings
- Disease recognition by farm staff: **daily assessment of bird behaviour**; appearance (feathering, size, uniformity, colour); environmental changes (litter quality, heat or cold stress, ventilation); clinical signs (respiratory noise or distress, depression, faecal droppings, vocalisation). ## 10. Vaccination, deworming, coccidiosis, withdrawal periods

### Takeaway
Hy-Line publishes a framework, not a universal schedule, and states that a single program cannot be recommended for all regions. Marek's is given at the hatchery. IBD (Gumboro) is given around days 15–32. Newcastle and IB get 2–3 live doses, with an inactivated dose 4–6 weeks after the last live one. Fowl pox and AE are given between weeks 6–15. Every vaccine date should be labelled "confirm with your local vet". For medicine withdrawal, follow the label or local legislation.

### Cited Findings
- All layer flocks should be vaccinated against Marek's, Newcastle, IB, IBD, AE and fowl pox. "A single program cannot be recommended for all regions." Follow the label, use approved vaccines, and consult local veterinarians. — [Hy-Line TU Vaccination Recommendations](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20VACC%20ENG.pdf)
- Marek's is given at the hatchery (Rispens or Rispens/HVT preferred). — [Hy-Line TU VACC](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20VACC%20ENG.pdf)
- IBD: given between **days 15–18, 21–25, 28–32** (live intermediate strains, timed by maternal-antibody decline), with drinking water preferred. — [Hy-Line TU VACC](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20VACC%20ENG.pdf)
- Newcastle (moderate challenge): **2–3 live vaccinations**, with **4–6 weeks** between the last live dose and the inactivated injection. At least one live dose in grow should be given by spray. Long-cycle flocks may need live boosters **every 30–60 days** in lay. — [Hy-Line TU VACC](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20VACC%20ENG.pdf)
- AE: given once between **6 and 15 weeks**. Fowl pox: by wing-web, 1–2 vaccinations; birds under 6 weeks need highly attenuated or pigeon pox vaccine. ILT: eye drop, not within 7 days of another live respiratory vaccine. Coryza and cholera: 2 doses 4 weeks apart where prevalent. — [Hy-Line TU VACC](https://www.hyline.com/Upload/Resources/TU%20VACC%20ENG.pdf)
- Marek's vaccination is recommended for all chickens at day one or in ovo. Fowl pox is advised only where previously present, at 12–16 weeks, before lay (veterinary hospital source, search summary). — [VCA Hospitals: healthcare for backyard chickens](https://vcahospitals.com/know-your-pet/healthcare-for-pet-and-backyard-chickens)
- Withdrawal: a withdrawal feed is required when regulated pharmaceutical feed additives are used, and local legislation sets the time. — [Ross Pocket Guide 2015](https://aviagen.com/assets/Tech_Center/BB_Resources_Tools/Pocket_Guides/Ross-Broiler-Pocket-Guide-2015-EN.pdf)

### Inferences
- The app should model vaccinations as user-entered events with a "confirm with your vet" label, not as hard-coded defaults. At most, it could offer Hy-Line's published windows as an optional template with `confidence: 'medium'` and the source attached.
- A medication entry should capture the label withdrawal days for meat and eggs, and block "eggs safe / slaughter OK" until they elapse (inference; no fixed number).
