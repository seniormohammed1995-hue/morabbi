# Information needs, problems and demands of household / backyard poultry keepers (Egypt and MENA first; Africa and South Asia for comparison)

Research note, Nov 2026. About 20 search/fetch calls. Several Egyptian EKB journal pages (journals.ekb.eg) refused automated fetches (ECONNRESET), so some Egyptian findings come from search-result abstracts and snippets, not full texts. Those are marked "[abstract/snippet only]". Where a search returned several EKB links without saying which finding came from which link, this is marked "[URL mapping uncertain]".

## Q1. Household poultry in Egypt: constraints, mortality, knowledge gaps, information sources

### Takeaway
Egyptian household poultry is mostly kept by rural women. Flocks are mixed-species (about 73 birds on average in one FAO-linked survey). The problems that come up again and again are early chick death (the 3–9 week window), disease (viral and bacterial, HPAI H5N1, zoonotic fears), feed and input cost, lack of cash, and poor access to services and inputs. Women's knowledge of extension recommendations is mostly low or medium. Their main information sources are family and neighbours, then vets and TV.

### Cited Findings
- Fasina, Ali, Yilma, Thieme and Ankers (FAO co-authors), *World's Poultry Science Journal* 72(1):178–188, 2016, "Production parameters and profitability of the Egyptian household poultry sector: a survey". Flocks are "multi-species", with a mean flock size of 73 mixed-breed birds. The main diseases are viral and bacterial. The sector is described as "women-driven". Mean net annual profit was 2,287.67 LE (US$397.34) per household. The abstract also says Egyptian household systems differ from other African systems in input systems, hatchery method and disease management — [Cambridge WPSJ](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/world-s-poultry-science-journal/article/production-parameters-and-profitability-of-the-egyptian-household-poultry-sector-a-survey/F7BA3B968E08C2DF57E77C3BF8B1F534) [abstract only; full text unavailable]
- A survey of Egyptian household flocks in three governorates found the highest mortality at 3–9 weeks of age (14%). A related summary gives mean chick mortality of 14.19% per year — [Academia.edu: Certain important production parameters in the Egyptian household poultry](http://www.academia.edu/20460186/CERTAIN_IMPORTANT_PRODUCTION_PARAMETERS_IN_THE_EGYPTIAN_HOUSEHOLD_POULTRY); [University of Pretoria repository](https://repository.up.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/949e9c15-4fd0-44f0-821a-20ffff4f2ed1/content) [snippet only; year not confirmed]
- A biosecurity cost-benefit survey (Fasina et al., with FAO) in Qalyubia, Gharbia and Menoufia found that basic biosecurity measures can be applied in household poultry. It estimated the benefit of biosecurity against H5N1 at 8.45 times that of doing nothing — [PMC5863456](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5863456) [snippet via search]
- An FAO livelihood study in Gharbia and Fayoum (16 villages) used interviews and focus groups with women poultry keepers to assess the household economic impact of HPAI — [FAO document](https://www.fao.org/3/aj033e/aj033e01.pdf) [snippet only]
- An FAO livelihoods document says 70% of backyard poultry in Africa and Asia is owned by women — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/3/aj033e/aj033e01.pdf) [snippet only]
- A study of rural Egyptian rearing and slaughter practices found that women raise household flocks, often helped by young children. Women over 15 accounted for 83% of the deaths among the Egyptian H5N1 cases it reports — [PMC4634263](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4634263/) [search snippet; the full page was blocked by a bot check]
- A later study says household poultry husbandry and slaughter are mainly female activities, and that women are often the main independent earners from these flocks. It built a behaviour-change tool for audiences with limited literacy — [PMC9826231](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9826231/)
- More than 90% of human H5N1 infections came from close contact with backyard poultry, and most clinical cases were women and children, who are the main caretakers. Women are often reluctant to report disease because they fear losing income and food — [PMC5863456 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5863456) [snippet via search]
- Kafr El-Sheikh (*Egyptian Journal of Agricultural Research*, 2015), 217 rural women: 63.59% had moderate or low knowledge of safe home poultry rearing. Their main information sources were family and neighbours, the veterinarian, TV programmes, and husband and sons — [EKB article 155488](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_155488.html) [abstract/snippet only]
- A Kafr El-Sheikh village study of 311 women found about 96% had low or medium knowledge of poultry extension recommendations. The main problems were early chick death (69%) and high prices (58%) — [EKB search results: article 298983 / 314595](https://ejar.journals.ekb.eg/article_298983.html) [URL mapping uncertain; snippet only]
- In a Gharbia village study of poultry extension needs, 27% of respondents had high needs, 58% average and 15% low. Needs were associated with age, poultry experience, flock owned, income, information sources and contact with extension — [EKB search results](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_314595.html) [URL mapping uncertain]
- Giza (2023), 341 women: knowledge of safe domestic poultry rearing was significantly correlated with years of education and with exposure to information sources — [EKB article 288973](https://mgiz.journals.ekb.eg/article_288973_5871e5b51cd4305e56d4a4e8788ef44f.pdf) [URL mapping uncertain; snippet only]
- In Fayoum, a study of 242 women who rejected improved chicken strains cited lack of information about the strains and negative attitudes towards them as factors — [EKB search results](https://journals.ekb.eg/article_314595.html) [URL mapping uncertain]
- Gharbia (Barma village, 100 households; *Annals of Agric. Sci. Moshtohor*): the main problems were lack of cash to spend on rearing, high chicken mortality and sudden death, diseases that pass from poultry to people, and poor availability of services and inputs — [Annals Moshtohor (EKB assjm)](https://assjm.journals.ekb.eg/article_104091_dda12e37364902d0f3b06cbd7b7d8d09.pdf) [Arabic snippet; URL mapping uncertain]
- Qalyubia (*Journal of Agricultural Economics and Social Sciences*, 2023): a random sample of 250 rural women was studied on their sources of home-poultry information and their use of the internet for it — [JAESS article 307037](https://jaess.journals.ekb.eg/article_307037_28c3f80bdc7f3d1211f8503a6fb01bc7.pdf) [the fetch failed; percentages not retrieved]
- A 2011 Egyptian study, citing FAO data, puts women's share of poultry rearing in Egypt at about 80%. It says most use primitive methods, which causes health problems (Mariout, Alexandria) — [EKB search results, Arabic](https://jaess.journals.ekb.eg/article_45404_c441f2193bcc00b3314ec1ee2578e5bb.pdf) [URL mapping uncertain]
- Rural women have limited access to agricultural information, technology and extension. Women's extension programmes focus mainly on home economics, including poultry raising — [AUC Social Research Center](https://www1.aucegypt.edu/src/wsite1/research/research_Agriculture.htm)
- A small-scale broiler profitability study in rural Egypt covers the challenges farmers face — [ResearchGate 371217885](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371217885_Factors_Affecting_Small-Scale_Broiler_Chicken_Farm_Profitability_and_Challenges_Faced_by_Farmers_in_Egyptian_Rural) [title only; no figures retrieved]
- Ministry of Agriculture summer guidance (aimed mainly at farms): cool the houses by spraying walls and roofs, remove feed during the hot hours, keep drinking water cool (add ice), and **postpone treatment and vaccination during heat stress**. Also reduce handling and light intensity, and move birds at night — [Al Mal News](https://almalnews.com/508854/«الزراعة»-تصدر-حزمة-إرشادات-لوقاية-المزارع-الحيوانية/); [Masrawy 2024](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2024/4/20/2570252); [Youm7 2020](https://www.youm7.com/story/2020/9/11/تعرف-على-إرشادات-الزراعة-لتغذية-ونقل-الماشية-والدواجن-لمواجهة-المناخ/4971049)
- The Animal Health Research Institute issued 15 tips for heatwaves (April 2026). They cover housing and humidity, balanced feeding and feed timing, and ventilation and cooling — [Masrawy, 23 Apr 2026](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/4/23/2977133/كيف-تحمي-حيواناتك-من-الحرارة-15-نصيحة-من-بحوث-الصحة-الحيوانية-)
- The Ministry of Agriculture launched a national awareness campaign on biosecurity for poultry keepers (Dec 2025) — [Maspero](https://www.maspero.eg/economy/2025/12/04/910568/الزراعة-تطلق-حملة-وطنية-لتوعية-مربيي-الدواجن-بإجراءات-الأمن-الحيوي)
- Assiut University study: giving Muscovy ducks access to an outdoor swimming pond for about 4 hours a day in hot summer improved performance, health and mortality compared with indoor-only rearing — [Assiut University](https://b.aun.edu.eg/agriculture/access-outdoor-swimming-pond-during-summer-season-improved-muscovy-ducks-performance-and-health-0)

### Inferences
- Across Egyptian village surveys, the most-cited problem is early chick mortality, followed by input and feed prices and disease. This points to brooding guidance for the first weeks, vaccination timing, and cheap feeding as priority content.
- Information flows mostly through family and neighbours, so the gap is credible and simple guidance. Low-literacy formats (icons, voice, Egyptian Arabic) matter, and PMC9826231 deliberately designed for limited literacy.
- Official summer advice says to postpone vaccination during heat stress. Weather-aware reminders that shift a vaccination date in a heatwave would fit official guidance.
- Fear of losing birds leads to under-reporting of disease, so a private, offline symptom-checking tool may meet a real need.

### Gaps
- I found no full-text, recent (2020+) Egypt-wide quantitative ranking of household keepers' information needs. The EKB full texts could not be fetched.
- I found no specific studies on rooftop rabbits, pigeons, turkeys or geese among household keepers' constraints. The Fasina 2016 species breakdown was not visible.
- No IFAD or World Bank Egypt household-poultry figures were retrieved.
- The rural Egyptian H5N1 practices paper (PMC4634263) could not be fully read, so details on rooftop housing and handling of dead birds are unconfirmed.

## Q2. Mobile/ICT use by smallholder poultry farmers: useful services, barriers, outcomes

### Takeaway
SMS calendars (iCow) and decision-support apps exist and farmers like them. But I found no rigorous trial showing that SMS vaccination reminders improve poultry outcomes. The strongest evidence is indirect: knowledge strongly predicts Newcastle disease (ND) vaccination, and the barriers are phone sharing, literacy, connectivity, language and distance to vaccine vendors. Voice and WhatsApp are preferred channels.

### Cited Findings
- iCow (Kenya) runs SMS "Kalenda" schedule-based reminder calendars, including Kienyeji (indigenous) chicken, broiler, layer and vaccination calendars. A 2012 self-reported study said 82% of farmers who joined seven months earlier were still on the platform, but the CGIAR clearing house lists its vetted performance as "None" — [CGIAR Digital Food Systems Evidence Clearing House](https://bigdata.cgiar.org/digital-intervention/icow-service/); [Engineering for Change](https://www.engineeringforchange.org/solutions/product/icow/)
- iCow has a database of about 600,000 farmers, with more than 60,000 active users — [iCow](https://icow.co.ke/.) [self-reported]
- About 178,000 SMS messages on zoonotic diseases were sent to 12,643 farmers, mainly in Kenya (CGIAR 2019) — [CGIAR performance report 2019](https://www.cgiar.org/annual-report/performance-report-2019/mobile-phones-combat-disease-in-east-africa)
- GALVmed partnered with iCow to use SMS for ND vaccination in Kenya. A farmer case study reports a self-estimated profit of about 125,000 KSh in five months; this is anecdotal — [GALVmed](https://www.galvmed.org/how-one-farmer-improved-his-life-and-farm-with-texting-2/)
- Nigeria SR-DISVAXFIC app (Pirbright/NVRI, Feb 2026), tested with 291 farmers in 5 northern states. Farmers found it intuitive, especially the income charts comparing vaccinated and unvaccinated scenarios. Many said it made them more likely to vaccinate, which is qualitative. Barriers: wariness of technology, no smartphone, and widely varying digital literacy. The app has no reminder feature — [Pirbright](https://pirbright.ac.uk/news/bridging-science-and-field-how-simple-app-supporting-animal-health-nigeria)
- Kwara State, Nigeria: 92% of poultry farmers used WhatsApp to share poultry information. Age, education and device cost shaped mobile use — [LMU eprints](https://eprints.lmu.edu.ng/id/eprint/5956)
- Nigerian poultry farmers prefer mobile phones and TV; educated farmers prefer computers and the internet — [JATLIM](https://jatlim.org/volumes/volume4/volume4%20no%201/Cajetan.pdf)
- Ibadan (180 egg farmers): ICT use was low, and phone use had a positive effect on technical efficiency — [AgEcon Search](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/347374/files/Effects%20of%20Information%20and%20Communication%20Technologies.pdf)
- Mobile decision-support system for poultry farmers in Tanzania — [ResearchGate 349756831](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349756831_Mobile-based_Decision_Support_System_for_Poultry_Farmers_A_Case_of_Tanzania) [no outcome numbers retrieved]
- Digital Green Ethiopia (dairy IVR, 1,057 farmers): women's reach was limited. About 4 in 10 female farmers shared a phone with family. Women cited work, poor network and language barriers. Recommendations were speakerphone listening and sending after 5 pm. The service's Net Promoter Score was 49 — [60 Decibels: female farmers in Ethiopia](https://60decibels.com/insights/female-farmers-in-ethiopia/); [Digital Green](https://digitalgreen.org/?p=1956)
- On Digital Green's AI chatbot, women made heavy use of local-language voice notes, especially women with lower literacy — [Agrilinks](https://agrilinks.org/post/leaping-gender-gaps-women-power-users-ai-chatbots-extension); in a multi-cohort study of AI advisory services, women gave higher satisfaction scores than men — [arXiv 2601.11537](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11537v1)
- In an India RCT with 1,006 farmers, text-message nudges substantially increased adoption of an agriculture app, while adoption in the control group stayed low — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214804326000650)
- Barriers to SMS reminders, from human child-vaccination work in Ethiopia: low phone ownership, network access, electricity for charging, and illiteracy. Suggested fix: voice calls or messages. Phone ownership was 88% in urban and 47.2% in rural households — [Dove Press JMDH](https://www.dovepress.com/acceptability-barriers-and-facilitators-of-mobile-text-message-reminde-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-JMDH)
- Makueni, Kenya (2020 data, published 2023; 1,274 households, 23 focus groups): only about 15% of households vaccinated against ND. The others used herbal remedies (58%), conventional drugs (10%) or nothing (16%). Each one-unit rise in the knowledge score (0–1 scale) raised the odds of vaccinating 32.5 times. 51% of non-vaccinators did not know ND vaccines existed. Only 24% had received any poultry information or training. Each extra km to a vendor cut the odds by about 4%. Minimum packs of 50 doses do not suit small flocks — [PLOS ONE 0283076](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0283076)
- Western Kenya (1,908 households, 2013–2017): mortality was 60% of all chicken off-take. 98% let birds scavenge, and 93% kept them inside the house at night. Only 12% reported any treatment, vaccination or deworming over 16 visits, and 1.35% used vet services for vaccination. Newcastle disease is the largest killer in small flocks. An outside estimate cited in the paper says about 70% of chicks die before 6 weeks in scavenging systems — [PMC7259595](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7259595/)
- In a western Kenya trial of routine ND vaccination, vaccination significantly increased flock size, but ND-preventable mortality was small compared with all-cause mortality — [PLOS ONE 0248596](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0248596)
- In Uganda (593 smallholders), ND vaccination was linked to an 80% increase in egg production after adjusting for hen numbers. The data rely on farmers' recall — [PMC7193891](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7193891/)
- GALVmed adoption study: flocks of good ND-vaccine adopters in Tanzania were larger (mean 29.9 chickens) than those of non-adopters (18.9). In Jharkhand, India, adopters had significantly fewer ND outbreaks — [GALVmed](https://galvdox.galvmed.org/download_file/6dc68ec1-bb60-4b92-b7f9-66f1e3da7a16/686)
- Producers in northern Ghana and central Tanzania had poor access to vet services and inputs — [PMC10352078](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10352078/)
- Bangladesh: an FAO SAPPLPP practice note (2020) describes community poultry vaccinators who brought vaccination, extension messages and disease surveillance to women poultry rearers — [FAO Family Farming Knowledge Platform](https://www.fao.org/family-farming/detail/en/c/1618397/)
- Ethiopia: a discrete-choice study of 450 smallholders' preferences for ND vaccination programmes notes that ND vaccination is not part of village-poultry extension in many developing countries — [PMC6152585](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6152585/)

### Inferences
- Knowledge and awareness are the main barriers to vaccination, ahead of cost. A tool that explains why and when to vaccinate, and reminds the user, targets the factor with the biggest measured effect (OR 32.5 in Makueni).
- Outside vaccination, all-cause mortality (chick losses, predation, nutrition, housing) is large. Daily-check and brooding guidance may matter as much as vaccination reminders.
- Design implications backed by evidence: offline use, voice or audio for low literacy, a shared family phone, evening timing, WhatsApp sharing, and local dialect.

### Gaps
- I found **no RCT or quasi-experiment of SMS or app vaccination reminders for poultry**, and no Egypt- or MENA-specific evaluation of a poultry ICT service.
- I found no GSMA mAgri report with poultry-specific figures.
- Bangladesh and India figures on mobile use by women poultry keepers were not retrieved.

## Q3 and Q4. Demand signals from Arabic online communities, and evidence on recurring reminders

### Takeaway
Searches did not surface accessible Arabic Facebook group or forum pages that could be cited. Public demand evidence for Egypt instead comes from heavy Arabic news coverage of official summer-heat and biosecurity guidance. I found no direct study showing that recurring reminders improve smallholder poultry outcomes. Support for them is indirect: human-health SMS-reminder RCTs, the India app-nudge RCT, and iCow's sustained use of its calendars.

### Cited Findings
- Egyptian Arabic news outlets often republish Ministry and Animal Health Research Institute heatwave advice for livestock and poultry. This shows recurring seasonal demand — [Masrawy 2026](https://www.masrawy.com/news/news_egypt/details/2026/4/23/2977133/كيف-تحمي-حيواناتك-من-الحرارة-15-نصيحة-من-بحوث-الصحة-الحيوانية-); [Al Mal News](https://almalnews.com/508854/«الزراعة»-تصدر-حزمة-إرشادات-لوقاية-المزارع-الحيوانية/); in the UAE, ADAFSA issues similar summer advice — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/uae/government/adafsa-urges-livestock-owners-to-safeguard-their-animals-during-summer-1.500228608)
- In human vaccination RCTs, SMS reminders produced a 39% relative increase in influenza vaccination in one trial — [Annals of Family Medicine](https://www.annfammed.org/content/15/6/507). A UK cluster trial found a non-significant absolute increase of 2.62% — [PMC4762100](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4762100/). This shows mixed but generally positive effects.
- iCow's calendars are an operating, long-running model of schedule-based reminders for chicken keepers — [CGIAR clearing house](https://bigdata.cgiar.org/digital-intervention/icow-service/)

### Inferences
- The topic list (summer heat, coccidiosis, Newcastle disease, egg drops, feed mixing, ducks and geese) is plausible but **not verified** here through citable Arabic community pages. The writer should treat it as a hypothesis, or rely on the Egyptian survey problems above: chick mortality, prices, disease and cash.

### Gaps
- No citable Arabic Facebook, forum or YouTube-comment evidence was found. Facebook groups are not indexed by the search tool, and the forum searches returned only English extension pages.
- I found no study that measures the effect of daily-check or vaccination reminders on smallholder poultry mortality or production.
