## Water: check and refill frequency, and species-specific needs

### Takeaway
All the sources assume water is available all the time; none gives a refill interval. For waterfowl the two key rules are: (1) they must be able to dip the whole bill or head, and (2) water must be kept off the litter (put drinkers on wire or slats). Open pools get dirty quickly and need flushing several times a day. Young poults, quail chicks and ducklings need drowning protection (marbles or screens) for the first 1–2 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- Ducks: fresh water is essential. Growing ducks do not need to swim. Pools or small ponds "get dirty quickly" and must be flushed and refreshed many times a day. Start ducklings on chick waterers, then move quickly to larger ones. Drip or nipple waterers over a wire-covered platform keep litter drier — [UW–Madison Extension, Care of Ducks](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/care-of-ducks)
- Ducklings and goslings need to submerge the whole bill to clean their nostrils, but the waterer should be shallow enough that they cannot climb in [snippet, secondary hatchery/supplier sources, LOW] — [The Poultry Site, Brooding and Rearing Ducklings and Goslings](https://www.thepoultrysite.com/articles/brooding-and-rearing-ducklings-and-goslings)
- Goslings: at least 2 cm of watering space per bird during brooding. Drinkers sit on wire mesh or slats to keep litter dry. 3 cm water depth is given for growing-stage drinkers. Water intake over the first 3 weeks is about 7–8 L per gosling — [FAO, goose production, brooding chapter](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4359E/y4359e0b.htm)
- Turkey poults: put marbles or foil balls in feeders and drinkers for the first 2 weeks, and dip beaks into water if needed. Drinker space: 0.5 in/poult (0–6 wk), 1.0 in (6–14 wk), 1.5 in (>14 wk) — [Penn State Extension, Small Flock Turkey Production](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Quail: cover waterers with a screen or put marbles in trays for the first 1–2 weeks to prevent drowning — [extension.org, Raising Japanese Quail](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-management/raising-japanese-quail-in-small-and-backyard-flocks/). Water space: 2 cm/adult quail and 1 linear cm/chick — [TNAU Agritech, Quail care & management](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)
- Pigeons: none of the fetched sources gives figures for bath water.

### Inferences
- Suggested reminders: "check and refill water" at least morning and evening for all species; for waterfowl, add a midday check in Egyptian summer, plus "flush the dipping or swimming pan" daily (Wisconsin says many times a day for pools). "Remove marbles or screen from drinkers" at 14 days (poults, quail chicks).
- Alert: "wet litter around drinker". Ducks are the worst case, per Wisconsin.

### Gaps
- No authoritative refill interval in hours, and no heat-day water intake figures for ducks, geese or turkeys.
- No sourced figures for pigeon bath frequency or water depth. Common practice is 1–2 baths a week, but that comes only from fancier/forum sources, so it is excluded.

## Brooding temperature by age, when to remove heat, and adult heat/cold stress

### Takeaway
Typical schedules start at 32–37 °C at bird level and drop about 3 °C (5 °F) per week. Bird behaviour overrides the thermometer: huddling or piling means too cold, panting or moving away from the heat means too hot. How long heat is needed: ducks 2–3 weeks in summer (5–6 in cold weather), goslings about 3 weeks, quail about 4 weeks, turkeys longest (no heat cut in the first 10 days, then 5 °F/week down to 70 °F).

### Cited Findings
- **Ducklings (Pekin-type)**: 85–90 °F (29–32 °C) at the edge of the heat lamp on arrival, then 5–10 °F lower each week. Use a chick guard for the first 3–4 days. Heat is needed 2–3 weeks in summer and 5–6 weeks in cold weather. Ducklings are feathered enough to go outdoors at about 4 weeks. Too hot: they move away. Too cold: they pile up and get noisy. High temperatures slow feathering and growth — [UW–Madison Extension](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/care-of-ducks)
- Missouri Extension gives about 90 °F at the hover edge [snippet] — [MU Extension G8920](https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/g8920)
- **Goslings**: 36–37 °C at bird level on arrival, 32–33 °C by the end of week 1, 23–25 °C by the end of week 2. No heat after week 3 unless the room is below 20 °C. Switch heat on at least 24 h before arrival; a guard is needed only for 2–3 days. Behaviour is the best guide — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4359E/y4359e0b.htm). NSW DPI: reduce gradually by 3–4 °C/week over 2–3 weeks [snippet] — [NSW DPI, Brooding and rearing goslings](https://www.dpi.nsw.gov.au/animals-and-livestock/poultry-and-birds/species/geese-raising/brooding-and-rearing)
- **Turkey poults**: heat the brooder at least 24 h before placement. Room about 88 °F (31 °C) and 95 °F (35 °C) under the heat source. Poults cannot regulate body temperature for the first 10 days; after that, lower 5 °F (about 3 °C) per week to 70 °F (21 °C). Brooder guards come out at about 2–2.5 weeks — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production). Other schedules: Kentucky starts at 100 °F at poult height, −5 °F/week [snippet]; Alberta 35 °C, −3 °C/week [snippet, 2004 document]; Utah State 95–98 °F at 3 in above the floor [snippet] — [Utah State](https://extension.usu.edu/poultry/research/basics-for-raising-junior-market-turkeys-for-junior-turkey-show), [Alberta Agdex 450/22-1](https://www1.agriculture.alberta.ca/$department/deptdocs.nsf/all/agdex3544/$file/450_22-1.pdf)
- **Japanese quail chicks**: start at 37 °C and lower 2.7 °C per week until 4 weeks of age. Keeping chicks caged through week 3 lowers early mortality — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html). The Poultry Site gives about 35 °C in week 1 and heat for 3–4 weeks [snippet, LOW]. Extension.org gives no temperature.
- **Pigeon squabs** (hand-reared only; parent-raised squabs need no heat): 35 °C on day 1 with 60% RH, −1 °C/day to 30 °C, then room temperature (22–24 °C) by 14–16 days — [Mohamed et al. 2025, BMC Vet Res 21:275](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998371/)
- Turkey heat management: ventilation of at least 0.5 cfm per lb of body weight. In high heat, about 400 cfm of air moving over the birds' heads helps — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)

### Inferences
- For the app, brooding temperature can be a per-species table (age in days → target °C), plus one behaviour-check reminder 2–3 times a day during brooding.
- Egyptian summer: Wisconsin's "2–3 weeks heat in summer" for ducklings suggests the app could shorten the heat period when ambient temperature exceeds the target. That fits the existing `src/calc/weather.ts` approach.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative adult heat-stress thresholds (°C or THI) for ducks, geese, turkeys, quail or pigeons. Merck and the breeder guides (Cherry Valley, Grimaud, Aviagen Turkeys) were not reached. Until sourced, use behaviour triggers (panting, wings held out, crowding at water) and any confidence:'low' values.
- No cold-stress thresholds for adults.
- No Muscovy or Mulard brooding schedule. Both are usually treated like Pekin, but I found no source for that.

## Feeding times per day, feed phase changes by age, and foods to avoid

### Takeaway
Extension sources say free-choice (ad libitum) feed at all times; none prescribes a number of meals a day. Phase changes: ducks at 2 and 6 weeks; turkeys at 3–4, 6, 10–12 and 12+ weeks; quail at 3 weeks and at laying (about 6–7 weeks); geese grazing from 2 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **Ducks** (NC State nutrient table, reproduced by Wisconsin): Starter 0–2 wk 20% CP; Grower 2–6 wk 18%; Finisher 6–8 wk 16%; Breeder developer 14.5%; Breeder layer 16% CP with 2.9% Ca. Starter crumble 0–2 wk, grower pellet from 2 wk. Free-choice feed at all times. Medicated chick starter is unnecessary. Intake is about 0.5 lb/duckling in week 1 and about 20 lb to market. Target market: 8+ lb at about 7 weeks — [UW–Madison Extension](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/care-of-ducks)
- Niacin requirement for ducks is about 55 mg/kg feed versus 27 mg/kg for chickens [snippet, feed-calculator site, LOW]. Merck has a Pekin nutrient-requirements table that I did not fetch — [MSD Vet Manual, Nutrient Requirements of Pekin Ducks](https://www.msdvetmanual.com/multimedia/table/nutrient-requirements-of-pekin-ducks)
- No benefit from more than about 16% protein after 2 weeks in a balanced duck feed [snippet] — [extension.org, Feeding ducks intended for consumption](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/feeds-and-feeding-of-poultry/feeding-ducks-intended-for-consumption/). Ohio State: never restrict feed during brooding [snippet] — [OSU Meigs 4-H](https://meigs.osu.edu/sites/meigs/files/imce/Feeding%20Ducks%20Union%20County%204H%20Ducks.pdf)
- **Geese**: waterfowl starter at 16–18% CP and 2,600–2,900 kcal ME/kg, ad libitum, for 3 weeks (about 2.5–2.7 kg/gosling). Extra feed on egg trays for the first 24 h. Feeder space 1.5 cm/bird. From 3 weeks, feed can be restricted to 75% of ad libitum. Grazing can start at 2 weeks in warm, dry weather; keep out of heavy cold rain until about 5 weeks — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4359E/y4359e0b.htm)
- **Turkeys**: 28% CP crumbles from 0 to 3–4 wk; 26% grower for the next 2 wk; 10–12 wk, 1 part grain to 3 parts 22% grower (about 19%); 12 wk to market, equal grain and grower (about 16%); never below 16%. Grit must be available before ranging or feeding whole grain. Medicated starter has a pre-slaughter withdrawal period printed on the feed tag. Feeder space: 1 in/poult (0–6 wk), 3 in (>6 wk) — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- **Quail**: chicks 0–3 wk at 27% CP and 2,750 kcal/kg; growers from week 4 at 24%; layers at 22% CP and 2,650 kcal/kg. Feeder space 2–3 cm/adult — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html). Grit/Grit.com gives starter at about 24% [snippet, LOW]. Sources conflict on starter protein (24–30%).
- **Foods to avoid**: the fetched sources give no list for these species. Mouldy feed is to be avoided (Wisconsin: keep litter free of mould). Medicated chick starter is unnecessary for ducks (Wisconsin).

### Inferences
- Phase-change reminders: duck day 14 (starter → grower) and day 42 (→ finisher, or developer for layers); turkey days 21–28, 35–42, 70 and 84; quail day 21 (→ grower) and day 42 (→ layer feed plus light program); goose day 14 (grazing allowed) and day 21 (optional restriction).
- Quail eggs are laid in the evening (see the egg section), which suggests an evening feed and water top-up and an evening egg collection.

### Gaps
- No authoritative "meals per day" for any species. Ad libitum is the extension norm.
- No authoritative pigeon ration or phase guidance was fetched.
- Foods to avoid (avocado, salty kitchen scraps, mouldy bread, etc.) were not confirmed from an extension source in this pass. The project's `src/data/scraps.ts` should keep its existing sources.

## Egg collection, nests, and the pigeon breeding cycle

### Takeaway
Quail lay mainly in the evening (6:30–7:30 pm), so collect daily in the evening or next morning. Ducks are said to lay in the early morning, but no fetched source confirmed this. The pigeon cycle is reminder-friendly: 2 eggs about 2 days apart, about 17–18 days of incubation, squabs weaned at 21–28 days, and the hen often re-lays around squab day 15. The full cycle is about 38 days (White Mirthys, Egypt) to about 52 days (Egyptian Baladi).

### Cited Findings
- Quail: eggs are laid from evening, mostly between 6:30 and 7:30 pm. Egg weight about 10 g. Sexual maturity at about 6 weeks (about 120 g). Male:female ratio 1:2. Fertile eggs can be collected from day 4 after the male is introduced; best fertility is in flocks up to 8 months old. Store hatching eggs at 14–16 °C and 70–80% RH and use within 7 days. Incubation 18 days at 36.9–37.2 °C and 60–70% RH; turn 4–6 times a day until day 14 — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)
- Quail start laying at about 7 weeks and lay economically for at least 1 year. Cages keep eggs cleaner; in community pens hens hide eggs in litter instead of nests — [extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-management/raising-japanese-quail-in-small-and-backyard-flocks/)
- Turkey hatching eggs: hold at 55–65 °F for no more than about 7–10 days. Set at 99.5–100 °F for 28 days and turn 3 times a day for 25 days — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Pigeon incubation is about 17–18 days [snippet, peer-reviewed reviews] — [PMC6523572](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6523572). Egyptian Baladi pigeons: second egg laid about 26–48 h after the first; egg-laying cycle about 52.75 days [snippet] — [Journal of Advanced Veterinary Research 2024](https://advetresearch.com/index.php/AVR/article/download/1626/1179). The hen may start laying again at about squab day 15 while the cock feeds the squabs [snippet] — [Archives Animal Breeding 68:395 (2025)](https://aab.copernicus.org/articles/68/395/2025/)
- Pigeons, Egyptian study (White Mirthys, open house, 17 h light): natural weaning at 28 days, with a 38.1-day interval between clutches. Squab weight 157 g at 7 d and 458 g at 28 d. Mortality 0–28 d was 3.8% with parent rearing and 26.7% when weaned at hatch. Traditional weaning is about 21–28 days — [Mohamed, Khattab & Elsagheer 2025, BMC Vet Res 21:275](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998371/) (note: a correction was published 9 Dec 2025)
- Muscovy incubation (about 35 days) and duck early-morning laying: not confirmed by any fetched source.

### Inferences
- Pigeon pair reminders, from the date the first egg is logged: day +2 "second egg expected"; day +17/18 "hatch expected" (count from the 2nd egg, since incubation usually starts then — LOW, unsourced); squab day 15 "hen may re-lay, check nest bowl"; squab day 21–28 "wean, move to young-bird section"; and an alert if no hatch by day 20 (LOW).
- Quail: daily evening egg-collection reminder, plus a "hatching eggs older than 7 days" alert.
- Turkey or quail incubation: "stop turning" reminder at day 25 for turkeys and day 14 for quail; "hatch" reminders at days 28 and 18.

### Gaps
- Duck laying time of day, nest box ratios, and age at first egg for Pekin, Muscovy and Mulard. Mulard is a sterile hybrid with no egg-breeding cycle (general knowledge, unsourced).
- Muscovy incubation of 35 days (versus 28 for Pekin) and goose incubation of 28–35 days were not confirmed this pass.

## Cleaning and bedding frequency; ammonia and wet litter

### Takeaway
Sources prescribe depth and spot-cleaning rather than a calendar. Use 3–5 in (about 8–12 cm) of absorbent, low-dust litter, remove wet patches, and top up as needed. Ducks foul litter fastest.

### Cited Findings
- Ducks: about 4 in of absorbent litter (shavings, chopped straw, peat). Wet litter is a bigger problem than with chicks: remove wet spots and add dry litter often, and keep it free of mould. Space: 0.5 ft²/bird for 2 weeks, ≥1 ft² by 4 weeks, ≥2 ft² if confined after the first month — [UW–Madison Extension](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/care-of-ducks)
- Turkeys: 3–5 in of clean, dry, low-dust bedding. No newspaper (slick, causes leg injuries). Remove wet areas and add litter as needed. Density: 1 poult/ft² for 5–6 weeks, then 3–5 ft²/bird from 8 weeks — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Geese: dry, absorbent, non-compacting litter (straw, shavings, rice hulls). Density 14–20/m² in week 1, 7–14 in week 2, 4–5 in week 3 — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4359E/y4359e0b.htm)
- Quail: adult cage space 150–180 cm²/bird; chicks 75 cm² under the hover plus 75 cm² in the run — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)
- Pigeons: in endemic areas, dust or dip with pyrethrin every 2 weeks for pigeon flies; deworm before breeding and racing seasons — [Harlin & Wade 2009, Vet Clin Exot Anim](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7110632)

### Inferences
- Reminders: daily "check for wet spots under drinkers" (ducks, geese); weekly "top up litter"; full clean-out between batches. Ammonia alert as a user check: smell at bird level or eyes stinging. No ppm threshold was sourced for these species.

### Gaps
- No ppm ammonia threshold or full-clean-out interval was sourced for these species.

## Lighting programs (quail and turkey)

### Takeaway
Laying quail need 14–18 h of light a day (TNAU says 16 h). Meat quail and non-breeding males get 8 h. Turkeys: dim light in week 1, then 12–14 h; bright spots cause piling and pecking.

### Cited Findings
- Quail: 14–18 h light/day to keep laying; 8 h for non-breeding males and meat quail — [extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-management/raising-japanese-quail-in-small-and-backyard-flocks/). Layers need a 16-h photoperiod — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html). An 8-h day delayed maturity; more than 16 h is recommended for laying females [snippet] — [Japanese Poultry Science (J-STAGE)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jpsa1964/26/4/26_4_235/_article/-char/en)
- Turkeys: lights dim in the first week, then 12–14 h is sufficient. Excess intensity or bright spots cause piling and cannibalism — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Pigeons (study condition only): 17 h light — [Mohamed et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998371/)

### Inferences
- A quail "start 16 h light" reminder at about 6 weeks, and an alert when daylight plus artificial light is under 14 h (Egyptian winter days are about 10–11 h, which needs a source before use).

### Gaps
- Commercial turkey lighting programs (Aviagen Turkeys / Hybrid) were not reached. No lux values.

## Weighing and growth checks

### Takeaway
Sourced weight checkpoints exist for Pekin ducks (8+ lb at about 7 weeks), turkeys (by strain and sex at 14 and 17 weeks), quail adults, and pigeon squabs (7 d and 28 d).

### Cited Findings
- Pekin: 8+ lb (about 3.6 kg) at about 7 weeks — [UW–Madison Extension](https://livestock.extension.wisc.edu/articles/care-of-ducks)
- Turkey live weight (lb) at 14 wk / 17 wk: White tom 25.2 / 36.0; White hen 20.6 / 27.7; Bronze tom 24.6 / 32.7; Bronze hen 19.9 / 25.2; Black tom 18.4 / 23.9; Black hen 13.7 / 16.9. Hens are marketed at about 14 wk, toms at about 17 wk — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Quail adults: males 100–140 g, females 120–160 g, jumbo 250–300 g — [extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-management/raising-japanese-quail-in-small-and-backyard-flocks/). Hatch weight 6–7 g; about 120 g at maturity (6 wk) — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)
- Pigeon squabs (parent-reared, White Mirthys): 157 g at day 7, 458 g at day 28 — [Mohamed et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11998371/)

### Inferences
- A weekly weigh-a-sample reminder (5–10 birds) works for meat ducks, turkeys and quail. Compare against the checkpoints above. Intermediate weekly curves are not sourced here.

### Gaps
- Weekly growth curves for Pekin, Muscovy, Mulard, geese and quail. Breeder guides (Cherry Valley, Grimaud) were not reached.

## Daily observation signs and common disease alerts

### Takeaway
Base the generic daily check on comfort behaviour and appetite. Species alerts with sourced signs: duck viral hepatitis (ducklings under 7 wk, sudden death with head thrown back), blackhead in turkeys (yellow droppings, listless, kept near chickens), pigeon PMV-1 (twisted neck, star-gazing, watery droppings), and pigeon canker (cheesy yellow mouth or throat lesions, weight loss).

### Cited Findings
- Comfort: birds spread evenly when comfortable, huddle when cold, and stay away from the heat when hot. Some losses are normal in the first 2 weeks. Get a lab diagnosis promptly if birds are sick — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production). FAO recommends frequent checks of comfort, feed and water for goslings — [FAO](https://www.fao.org/4/Y4359E/y4359e0b.htm)
- **Duck viral hepatitis**: ducklings typically under 6 weeks; no clinical signs over 7 weeks. Incubation 18–48 h. Signs: lethargy, loss of balance, spasmodic paddling, and death within minutes, typically with opisthotonos (head bent back). Mortality up to 95%, nearly all within 1 week of the first signs. Strictly isolate ducklings for the first 5 weeks; avoid wild waterfowl; control rats — [Merck Vet Manual, Duck Viral Hepatitis](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/poultry/duck-viral-hepatitis/duck-viral-hepatitis)
- **Histomoniasis (blackhead)** in turkeys: signs appear 7–12 days after infection, including listlessness, poor appetite, drooping wings, unkempt feathers, and yellow (sulfur-coloured) droppings. The head does not turn black. Mortality is commonly 80–100%. Spread via eggs of the cecal worm *Heterakis gallinarum* and via earthworms; chickens are carriers. Raise turkeys apart from chickens; there are no approved drugs or vaccines (US) — [Merck Vet Manual, Histomoniasis](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/poultry/histomoniasis/histomoniasis-in-poultry). Avoid ground used by chickens in the previous 3–5 years — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- **Pigeon PMV-1**: neurological signs are stargazing, torticollis (twisted neck) and polyuria (watery droppings). Incubation is given as 1–6 weeks or 2–4 weeks — [Harlin & Wade 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7110632). In pigeons with virulent NDV: conjunctivitis, rhinitis, dyspnoea, diarrhoea, tremors, ataxia, torticollis — [Merck, Newcastle Disease in Poultry](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/poultry/newcastle-disease-and-other-avian-paramyxovirus-infections/newcastle-disease-in-poultry)
- **Pigeon trichomonosis (canker)**: cheesy accumulations in the mouth or throat and weight loss. More common in pigeons and doves than in poultry. Passed from parents to squabs in crop milk; separate chronic carriers from breeders — [Merck, Trichomonosis in Birds](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/trichomonosis/trichomonosis-in-birds)
- Other pigeon signals: circovirus affects birds 2 months to 1 year old (death in 3–5 days). Coccidia deaths peak at 3–4 months of age. Quarantine new birds and introduce one pair at a time — [Harlin & Wade 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7110632)
- Quail are "fairly disease resistant" — [extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-management/raising-japanese-quail-in-small-and-backyard-flocks/). Quail (ulcerative) enteritis is the main disease; treat on a vet's advice — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)

### Inferences
- Red-flag alerts ("call a vet now"): sudden ducklings deaths or paddling or head thrown back under 7 weeks; turkeys with yellow droppings plus drooping wings; pigeons with a twisted neck or star-gazing (PMV-1 is notifiable in many countries); yellow mouth lesions in squabs or parents.
- Daily generic check: eating and drinking, activity, droppings, breathing, any bird alone or fluffed up.

### Gaps
- Signs for goose-specific diseases (e.g. goose parvovirus/Derzsy's disease) and duck plague, and Muscovy-specific issues, were not researched in this pass.

## Vaccination timing and medication withdrawal

### Takeaway
Programs exist for duck hepatitis (breeders and day-olds) and pigeon PMV-1. Turkeys in small flocks usually need none unless there is local disease history. Quail usually need none. Every program should say "your local vet decides". Withdrawal periods: follow the feed tag or label.

### Cited Findings
- Duck viral hepatitis: live DHAV-1 vaccine for breeders subcutaneously at 16, 20 and 24 weeks, then every 12 weeks through lay. Inactivated booster before lay in primed breeders. Day-old ducklings from non-immune breeders get live vaccine (SC or foot-web stab); immunity develops in 3–4 days — [Merck](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/poultry/duck-viral-hepatitis/duck-viral-hepatitis)
- Turkeys: vaccines may not be necessary in small flocks unless there is a disease history on the farm or nearby. Medicated starter has a withdrawal period on the feed tag — [Penn State](https://extension.psu.edu/small-flock-turkey-production)
- Pigeons: oil-emulsion PMV-1 vaccine SC 1–2 months before breeding and 6–8 weeks before racing or shows. Young birds are vaccinated at weaning; new birds during quarantine. Pox vaccine from 4 weeks of age, annually about 4 weeks before mosquito season, with immunity in 3–4 weeks. Deworm before breeding and racing seasons. Drug doses in this 2009 review are not current dosing advice — [Harlin & Wade 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7110632)
- Quail: routine immunisation is not needed — [TNAU](https://agritech.tnau.ac.in/ta/animal_husbandry/ani__quail_care&mgt.html)

### Inferences
- Reminders that need no vet input: pigeon "PMV-1 booster due" about 6 weeks before the user's breeding start date; "vaccinate weaned squabs" at squab day 28; "deworm before breeding". For ducks and turkeys, only a "talk to your vet about vaccinations" reminder in week 1, with no dates.
- Do not put drug doses in the app.
